Guardrails

Limitations: what these numbers cannot tell you

  • A comparison, not a forecast. Fit on 2017–2018 only, the model predicted 2019 member counts in the discounting regions within about {{ facts.backcast_treated_pct }}% (the first baseline we planned missed by {{ facts.backcast_same_season_pct }}%) and missed Panem by about {{ facts.backcast_control_pct }}%. Use it to rank policies.
  • Offering everyone a discount is untested. Offers reached about {{ facts.tested_offer_share_pct }}% of prospects in the data. The simulator assumes people respond the same at full coverage, which is why the recommendation keeps a randomized holdout.
  • The base-price cut is not cleanly identified. One comparison region and non-parallel trends, so its size is not claimed.
  • Tested prices only. ${{ facts.prospect_low }}–${{ facts.prospect_high }} for new customers and ${{ facts.member_low }}–${{ facts.member_high }} for members, at a ${{ facts.base }} base. Anything outside is flagged as an extrapolation.
  • Pull-forward is described, not measured. Among prospects who did not join, a deeper offer is followed by slightly lower sign-up next quarter (about {{ facts.pull_forward_low_pct }}–{{ facts.pull_forward_high_pct }}% of the gain), but that comparison is not causal. Customers gaming win-back offers cannot be seen at all.
  • Every quarter looks like an average 2019 quarter: no seasonality or marketing changes. Revenue is net of discounts; content costs are out of scope.
