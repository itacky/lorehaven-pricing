Guardrails

Limitations: What these numbers cannot tell you

  • Broader discount coverage is untested. Historically, about {{ facts.tested_offer_share_pct }}% of prospects received a discount. The simulator assumes customers would respond the same way if discounts reached most prospects. Test that by expanding coverage in stages and keeping a randomized holdout; a {{ facts.holdout_arms['list'] }}% list-price group can detect a {{ facts.mde_renewal_pts }}-point renewal gap within three quarters.
  • The $12 and $15 base prices were used in different years. Sign-ups may have changed for reasons other than price, and the gap to Panem also moved before the cut. Test base prices at the same time in comparable markets, with the same discount rules.
  • Some price choices need more evidence. The tool blends the $12 and $15 response rates to estimate $13 and $14, and it excludes full-price renewals because every member in the discounting regions received a discount. Test these choices before adopting them.
  • The simulator can't see customers gaming discounts. It doesn't capture customers who cancel to qualify for a win-back discount or wait for a deeper one. Track repeat cancellations and discount use, and test a cooldown period.
  • Each scenario repeats the same response rates every quarter. At the ${{ facts.base }} base the simulator uses average 2019 rates; other bases use earlier rates or a blend. Projections don't account for seasonality or changes in marketing, so use them to compare policies rather than to forecast. Revenue is net of discounts; content costs are out of scope.
